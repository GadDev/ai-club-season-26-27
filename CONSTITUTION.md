# Constitution

1. **The programme is the product.** The application supports good sessions and cannot consume the energy needed to make them.
2. **Show the whole season honestly.** The programme and session details are public. Publish proposals early, but never portray an unconfirmed date or venue as booked.
3. **Keep the tracks parallel.** Foundations, Engineering, and Deep Dive are concurrent entry points, not grades or sequential stages.
4. **Separate dimensions.** Track is audience; domain is subject; format describes teaching; date is schedule; status expresses the editorial lifecycle.
5. **Be rigorous at every level.** Beginner assumes no prior knowledge of this subject. Advanced depth must serve a meaningful problem.
6. **Start with the timeline.** The first release is a readable programme, not an account system, voting product, media library, or organizer dashboard.
7. **Favor practice and critical thinking.** Use examples, demonstrations, exercises, failure analysis, and trade-offs; avoid hype.
8. **Keep content portable and accountable.** Use standard files, stable links, source credit where appropriate, and reviewable corrections. Add public presenter credit only after it is approved.
9. **Build accessibility into V1.** Keyboard access, readable text, sufficient contrast, and a list equivalent to a visual timeline are required. Never rely on color alone.
10. **Make voting fair when introduced.** Verify eligibility with SFEIR identity, enforce one effective choice per eligible account on the server, explain how choices affect selection, and retain only the information needed to administer and audit a vote. Do not expose individual choices publicly without an explicit reason and consent.
11. **Respect participants and presenters.** Avoid attendance surveillance, streaks, leaderboards, and personal scores. V1 omits presenter identity; later public recognition is approved, professional, and not competitive.
12. **Publish materials with permission.** Presentations and replays need author and participant permissions, an audience policy, and a reliable place to live. Do not treat a public link as access control.
13. **Protect editorial independence.** Future SFEIR Institute links should be contextual and clearly identified.
14. **Prefer the smallest maintainable system for the current phase.** Use static Git-managed content for the timeline. Add identity, persistence, and media infrastructure when voting and replay requirements are concrete.

**Review question:** Does a proposed change make the current phase easier to understand, operate, or trust? If it conflicts with a principle, record the reason in the review.
